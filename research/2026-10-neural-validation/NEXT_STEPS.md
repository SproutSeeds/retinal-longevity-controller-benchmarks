# Existing-data recovery and the next human measurement

The immediate priority is to recover missing metadata for observations that already exist. The nine-donor neural result is sufficiently consistent to carry into a replication specification. The human marker analyses define measurement sensitivities that any later experiment or model must handle. Neither result removes the matched late human recovery gap.

## What would count as the next scientific advance

For the acute neural result, a new dataset must provide independent donors, paired cultures, an appropriate drug control, documented processing order and a prespecified cell-cycle summary. Replication should report every donor, gene concentration and normalization sensitivity. Functional proliferation/viability measurements would be needed to connect the RNA pattern to cell behavior. An independent study is needed before claiming broader reproducibility or field novelty.

For the human project, the target is an interpretable joint observation: age improvement with retained adult identity, calibrated detection of unusual states and persistence after verified factor withdrawal. A donor-matched snapshot can support population-level recovery. A claim that the same cells recovered requires lineage tracking or another justified longitudinal linkage. Fibroblast evidence supplies a feasibility stage; an ocular claim subsequently needs the relevant human retinal/RPE model and its function.

The strongest public story at this stage is the reproducible result with its failure boundaries: a consistent acute neural score, a corrected human marker interpretation and an explicit route to recover unused observations. The work does not establish a biological breakthrough or a clinically useful protocol.

## Conditional funding package

The existing [measurement plan](https://github.com/SproutSeeds/retinal-longevity-controller-benchmarks/blob/main/research/2026-10-public-data/MEASUREMENT_PLAN.md) remains the starting design. It proposes three-donor feasibility with 11 collections per donor (**33 collections**), or a fuller timing ladder with 19 per donor (**57 collections**). An optional late endpoint adds six collections. These are planning counts, not power calculations or a laboratory protocol.

The feasibility question is whether compatible measurements can distinguish recovery from persistent identity loss while keeping age and unusual-state assessments interpretable. Before a funded run, a qualified experimental collaborator should finalize the biological system, exact induction/withdrawal schedule, orthogonal viability/identity assays and practical sampling plan. Processing should be balanced across donors and arms, with retained sample/plate/well/time metadata. Replicate and donor counts should follow estimated between-donor variability and assay precision rather than the illustrative three-donor count alone.

This extension adds five requirements to that package:

1. Preserve gene-level contributions and fixed panel denominators. CD24 or Nanog dependence must remain visible.
2. Calibrate unusual-state detection with appropriate positive and negative reference material; measure false-positive behavior across depth. A marker threshold alone cannot estimate true rare-state prevalence.
3. Prespecify cell/feature support, retained denominators and depth sensitivities before treatment outcomes. Report each donor separately.
4. Validate the age estimator on its intended cell type and processing pipeline, with an external reference prediction. Keep its training data separate from evaluation donors and studies.
5. Use orthogonal identity, viability and functional endpoints to test the biological meaning of transcriptional scores and the persistence of the result after withdrawal.

A funding submission can include this public repository, the six-slide presentation, frozen analysis contracts, source-recovery evidence and an assay-by-assay measurement matrix. The budget should be filled from actual institutional/core quotes for cell acquisition, handling, assays, sequencing, analysis and contingency. No prices, power guarantee, partner commitment or wet-lab booking are implied here.

## Synthetic data and model training

Synthetic adversaries and count thinning already test software failure modes and measurement sensitivity. Further simulations are justified when they decide a specific sampling or calibration choice using stated assumptions. They cannot label an unobserved biological outcome as successful recovery.

A custom model becomes useful when a compatible, licensed target exists: for example, a calibrated identity/unusual-state classifier evaluated on held-out donors and an independent study. The current 57,317 barcodes come from two donors, and treatment-associated patterns are not validated safety labels. Training a larger model on these proxies would not resolve that gap. Freeze labels, splits, baseline methods and acceptance criteria before training; require an improvement over the fixed-marker baseline on external observations.

The next execution cycle begins when one of these prerequisites changes. It should end with a reproducible comparison and a clear accept, narrow or reject decision. Repeating analyses on unchanged data cannot supply the missing biological measurements.

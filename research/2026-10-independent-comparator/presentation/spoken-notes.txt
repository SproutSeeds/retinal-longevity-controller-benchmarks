1. Human expression comparison

I analyzed the eight human libraries under a fixed contract. All 46 predefined marker genes were present. Fibroblast markers decreased, while cycle and reprogramming markers increased under both normalization methods. The OSK stress score changed direction between methods. That makes a simple stress-benefit interpretation unreliable. The reprogramming row excludes POU5F1 and SOX2 because the treatment introduces those factors. These are descriptive expression changes from one donor. They do not establish rejuvenation, identity preservation or safety. The public report includes the full panel and every sensitivity.

2. Neural data admission

I verified the deposited mouse neural object against its repository checksum, raw count columns and the authors’ animal map. The seven animals include two old treated, three old untreated and two young untreated mice. The deposited subset omits all withdrawal samples. Both young animals occur in lane three, which limits age comparisons. Four numeric clusters totaling seventy cells still lack biological labels. This pass verifies that an animal-level analysis is possible. I have not yet run neural expression contrasts. The next contract will keep expression within a cell type separate from changes in the mix of recovered cells.

3. Synthetic donor balance

The simulation keeps the total at twelve hundred cells. Under the displayed hypothetical assumptions, balanced sampling across six donors yields about a sixty-eight percent chance of flags in at least two donors. An imbalanced six-donor allocation yields about forty-two percent. Requiring two donors reduces false alarms, but it also loses sensitivity. With no true unusual states and ninety-nine point nine percent specificity, the balanced design still has about a thirty percent chance of two flagged donors when errors are independent. The detector needs empirical calibration. These simulations guide analysis design; they do not create biological observations or establish a sample size.
